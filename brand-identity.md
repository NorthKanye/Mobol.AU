# Mobol — Brand Identity & Voice

> **Purpose of this file.** A single source of truth for how Mobol sounds, what we believe, and the words we use (and avoid). Every piece of copy on the site — hero, services, values, contact, meta tags — should pass the checks in this document before it ships.
>
> **Last updated:** 19 May 2026

---

## 1. Who we are (in one paragraph)

Mobol is an Aboriginal-owned digital agency based in Perth, Western Australia, working with clients across Australia. We design and build websites, brands, web applications, and AI integrations for small businesses, growing teams, and community organisations. We're not the biggest agency in town, and we don't want to be — we want to be the one that does honest work, delivers what was promised, and treats the people we work with as partners.

### The shorter versions

- **One-liner:** A Perth-based, Aboriginal-owned digital agency for websites, brands and AI — built on honest scope, fair pricing, and real partnership.
- **Six words:** Honest digital work, done in partnership.
- **Two words:** Considered work.

### Words we use to describe ourselves

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| digital agency · design and development team · Aboriginal-owned agency · Perth-based team | digital studio · creative house · agency-of-the-future · boutique anything |

"Agency" is the default. If "agency" feels too corporate in a sentence, just describe what we do ("we design and build websites, brands, and AI integrations…") and skip the noun.

## 2. Personality

Mobol's voice is **calm, considered, modern, and capable**. We sound like people who care about the work, know the tools, and have nothing to hide. We don't perform experience we don't have, and we don't undersell the work we *can* do.

| Trait | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Calm, considered, polished | Loud, hyped, urgent |
| Posture | Modern and professional | Stiff or corporate |
| Confidence | Quiet, earned by the work itself | Boastful, or hedged with disclaimers |
| Humour | Dry, used sparingly | Quirky, cutesy, meme-y |
| Pace | Measured | Frantic |
| Energy | Grounded | Performative |

### What we don't do

- **We don't overclaim experience.** No "decades of," no "veteran team," no inflated client lists. The work and the way we run a project speak for us.
- **We don't underclaim either.** No "we're a small team just starting out," no "thanks for taking a chance on us." We do this work properly. We charge fairly for it. That's the posture.
- **We don't lean on numbers we haven't earned.** If we put a metric on the site ("X projects delivered," "Y on-budget"), it's real and current. Until then, we describe how we work, not how much we've done.

If a sentence is trying to make us sound bigger, older, or more impressive than we are — cut it. If a sentence is apologising for being newer — cut that too.

## 3. Voice & tone rules

**Polished and professional — but never cold, and never performative.** Write the way a thoughtful, modern collaborator would speak in a first meeting with a client they respect. Grown-up, clear, no jargon for jargon's sake.

### Default settings

1. **Plain English first.** If a sentence could confuse a tradie *and* a tech lead, rewrite it. Both need to understand us.
2. **Short sentences. Real verbs.** Cut adjectives that aren't doing work.
3. **Active voice.** "We build it" beats "it gets built."
4. **One idea per sentence.** Two if they're related and short.
5. **Specific over vague.** "Three weeks" beats "quickly." "Fixed price" beats "competitive rates."
6. **Earn every adjective.** "Custom," "modern," "premium" — only if the work proves it on the same page.

### Cadence

We mix short, declarative lines with the occasional longer one for warmth. Avoid lists of three adjectives ("modern, sleek, beautiful"). Avoid sentences that start with "We are passionate about…".

### Dry wit, sparingly

A wry aside is welcome once or twice per page when it lands. Never forced. If it makes someone smile or nod, keep it. If it makes them roll their eyes, cut it. We're not a comedy account.

## 4. Values (existing — do not rename)

Names are locked in the UI. The copy underneath each is written in the new voice and based on what you actually mean by each value.

### 01 — Walking Together
*Growth and collaboration, side by side.*

We don't deliver work *to* clients — we walk the path with them. Real partnership: regular conversations, decisions made together, room for the brief to grow as we both learn. When the project's done, we want both sides to have moved forward, not just the website.

**Short form (≤140 chars):**
> Real partnership. We walk the path with you — open conversations, shared decisions, growth on both sides.

### 02 — Innovation
*Modern tools, used early — without being reckless.*

Modern frameworks, modern AI, the better way of doing things. We don't take years to adopt new tools when the new ones are clearly better. We stay current, learn fast, and bring that into client work — so the things we build are ahead of where most of the market is sitting.

**Short form (≤140 chars):**
> Modern frameworks, modern AI, newer tools as soon as they earn their place. We stay current so your work doesn't go stale.

### 03 — Trust
*Honesty and integrity, in every conversation.*

We tell you what's worth building and what isn't — even when it means a smaller project. Fixed prices where we can. Clear timelines. No surprise invoices. If something will cost more or take longer, you hear it from us first, not after the fact.

**Short form (≤140 chars):**
> Honest scope. Honest pricing. We tell you what's worth building — and what isn't.

### 04 — Unity
*Building Aboriginal-led business, together.*

We work to grow strong, Aboriginal-led businesses — ours and the ones we partner with. That means hiring, upskilling, and collaborating with mob across the industry. It's how we operate, not a line on the website.

**Short form (≤140 chars):**
> Hiring, upskilling, and partnering with mob. Building strong Aboriginal-led business — ours and the ones we work alongside.

## 5. How we talk about the Aboriginal identity

**Principle:** quietly present, lived not led with. The work and the craft are the headline. Identity is part of the foundation — visible where it matters, never the only thing we say about ourselves.

### Where it shows up
- About page — clearly stated, with context.
- Capability statements and procurement responses — front and centre (it matters there).
- Homepage — the existing "Aboriginal-owned. People-led." pill stays. We don't lead the hero headline with it.
- Footer / contact — a short Acknowledgement of Country (see §6).

### How to write it
- **Use:** "Aboriginal-owned," "Aboriginal-owned agency," "an Aboriginal-owned team in Perth."
- **"Indigenous-owned"** is acceptable in SEO contexts (procurement and search queries use it), but in body copy prefer "Aboriginal-owned" — it's more specific and more respectful per Reconciliation Australia guidance.
- **Don't use:** marketing-y phrasing that turns identity into a sales hook ("Choose us because we're Indigenous!"). Identity is context, not pitch.
- **Supply Nation:** registration is in progress. Once certified, add the badge and "Supply Nation certified" line to the footer, About, and capability statement. Do not claim it until it's live.

## 6. Cultural sensitivity — house rules

This section is a working summary. The full guidance is in the sourced documents linked at the bottom. **When in doubt, consult a Traditional Owner or community member — written guides don't replace that conversation.**

### Capitalisation
- **Always capitalise** "Aboriginal," "Torres Strait Islander," "Country" (when referring to ancestral lands), "Elder," "Traditional Owner," "Traditional Custodian," "First Nations," "Sea," "Sky" (when used in place of Country), and the names of Peoples or language groups (Noongar, Whadjuk, Yamatji, etc.).
- "Indigenous" is capitalised when referring specifically to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples in Australia.

### Terms to use
- **"Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples"** (note the plural "Peoples" — it reflects the diversity of nations, language groups, and cultural identities, not a single homogenous group).
- **"First Nations"** — acceptable, especially in inclusive contexts.
- **"Mob"** — culturally specific; appropriate for use *by* Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people about their own community. Since Mobol is Aboriginal-owned, the existing site copy ("keep us moving as one mob") is appropriate from us. Don't put it in client-voice copy or testimonials unless the speaker is also First Nations.
- **"Aunty," "Uncle," "tidda"** — only used by First Nations people, or with explicit permission. We don't put these in client-voice copy.

### Terms to avoid
- **"ATSI"** — don't abbreviate. It's widely considered disrespectful. Always write it out.
- **"Aborigine," "the Aborigines"** — outdated and depersonalising. Use "Aboriginal person/people/Peoples."
- **"Half-caste," "full-blood," "quarter-caste"** — assimilationist, deeply offensive, never used.
- **"Our Aboriginal people," "our Indigenous brothers and sisters"** — possessive framing. Avoid.
- **"Pre-history," "discovered Australia," "settled"** — frame Australia as terra nullius. Use "before colonisation," "invaded," "colonised," or factual dating ("for over 65,000 years").
- **Deficit framing** — "disadvantaged Aboriginal communities," "the Aboriginal problem." Always use strengths-based language.

### Strengths-based language
Frame Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, businesses, and communities by their **strengths, agency, and contribution** — never as a problem to be solved or a population in need of help. We're an Aboriginal-owned business growing within an industry. That's the frame.

### Acknowledgement of Country (footer)

Mobol's office is on **Whadjuk Noongar boodja** (Perth). We include an Acknowledgement of Country in the footer of every page.

**Use this wording (or close to it):**
> Mobol is based on Whadjuk Noongar boodja. We acknowledge the Whadjuk people of the Noongar Nation as the Traditional Custodians of this land, and pay our respects to Elders past and present.

**Things to know:**
- An **Acknowledgement of Country** is something *anyone* can give — including non-Indigenous people and organisations. We can include this on our site ourselves.
- A **Welcome to Country** can *only* be performed by a Traditional Owner of the land in question. We don't write or perform Welcomes ourselves, and we don't put a "Welcome to Country" on the website. If a client event needs one, we connect them with the appropriate community contact.
- The wording above is sincere, specific, and current. Don't make it longer or more flowery. Don't shorten it to "We acknowledge the Traditional Owners" without naming the Whadjuk people — generic acknowledgements feel like a tick-box.
- If the business address moves out of Whadjuk Noongar boodja, the acknowledgement must be updated to name the correct Traditional Custodians of the new location.

### Imagery, design, and cultural materials
- **No Aboriginal art on the site that isn't created by an Aboriginal artist and licensed to us in writing.** No AI-generated "Aboriginal-style" art, no stock-photo dot paintings, no Aboriginal-style patterns generated by us or a third party.
- **The Aboriginal Flag** (Harold Thomas) and **Torres Strait Islander Flag** (Bernard Namok) are credited wherever they appear. The existing values section already does this — keep that pattern.
- If we ever commission cultural artwork or symbols, the artist is credited by name and Nation on every page where their work appears. Payment terms and licensing are agreed in writing.

### Project work for clients
When we deliver work for Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander clients, the same rules apply to *their* site. We never source unlicensed Aboriginal artwork on a client's behalf. If a client wants cultural imagery, we help them connect with and pay an appropriate artist.

### When to ask, not assume
- Naming a specific Nation or language group on a page — ask the client or check with a community contact.
- Spelling of place names in language (e.g. "boodja" vs alternative spellings) — defer to the current preference of the local community.
- Using a cultural term, story, or motif — ask first, always.

## 7. Who we're talking to

In rough order of weight on the homepage:

1. **WA small businesses and sole traders.** Trades, cafes, professional services, consultants. First or second proper website. Modest budget. Want it done right and explained in plain English.
2. **Aboriginal organisations, community groups, and not-for-profits.** Mission-driven. Often grant-funded. Need a partner who understands the cultural and operational context, not just a vendor.
3. **Growing AU SMBs and startups.** Past the scrappy stage. Need a real site, a brand system, sometimes AI tooling. Project budgets A$10–80k+.

**Writing test:** every paragraph on the homepage should make sense to all three. If it only speaks to the startup founder, it's wrong. If it only speaks to the tradie, it's wrong.

## 8. What we believe

A short list of opinions we hold. Useful for tone, About copy, and decision-making when we're not sure how to phrase something.

- **Most websites are oversold and underdelivered.** Honest scope beats a flashy proposal.
- **A site you can update yourself is worth more than a beautiful site you can't.**
- **AI is a tool, not the product.** We use it where it earns its place.
- **Design and code aren't separate disciplines.** They have to be made together.
- **Small businesses deserve the same quality of work as big ones.** The budget is different. The care isn't.
- **Aboriginal business growth is a long game.** We hire, partner, and upskill inside the industry, not just deliver client work.

## 9. Words we use / avoid

### Use (yes)
work, build, design, ship, deliver, partner, considered, honest, fair, plain, clear, modern, solid, real, grounded, agency, team, craft, brief, scope, in plain English, in practice.

### Avoid (no)
solutions, holistic, synergies, unlock, leverage, future-proof, ecosystem, robust, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, passionate, journey (the brand kind), bespoke (overused), pixel-perfect, ninja/guru/wizard, transform your business, take it to the next level, 10x, crush it, hustle, digital studio, creative house, boutique.

### Handle with care
- **"Premium"** — fine, but earn it on the same page with specifics. Otherwise it reads like an empty claim.
- **"Custom"** — use only when the alternative is actually a template. Don't dilute it.
- **"AI"** — say what kind. "AI integrations," "an AI assistant for your team," "AI-powered search." Not the bare word as a status symbol.
- **"Aboriginal-owned"** — context, not slogan. Don't pair it with hype words.
- **"Indigenous"** — fine for SEO and procurement language. In body copy, prefer "Aboriginal" or "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander" — more specific, more respectful.

### Cliché replacements

| Cliché | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Take your business to the next level" | "Help your business do more, better." |
| "Unlock your potential" | (cut the sentence) |
| "Cutting-edge solutions" | "Modern tools, used where they earn it." |
| "We're passionate about…" | "We care about…" or just delete |
| "Bespoke digital solutions" | "Websites and apps, built for you." |
| "Future-proof your website" | "Built so it's easy to update later." |
| "We deliver world-class results" | (show a result instead) |
| "Empower your team" | "Give your team the tools to…" |

## 11. SEO direction

**Geography:** Perth-first, WA second, national third. Always include "Perth" or "Perth, WA" in page titles, meta descriptions, the homepage hero context, and the footer. Don't sound like a hyperlocal-only agency in the body copy — work flows in from across Australia.

### Primary keyword clusters

**Service + location**
- web design Perth · web developer Perth · web development Perth WA
- branding agency Perth · brand designer Perth
- AI integration Perth · AI consultant Perth
- digital agency Perth · web app development Perth

**Aboriginal / Indigenous-owned variants** (matter for procurement, Supply Nation searches, IPP queries)
- Aboriginal-owned digital agency · Indigenous-owned web agency
- Aboriginal-owned web design Perth · Indigenous-owned web developer Australia
- Aboriginal-owned agency Perth WA

### Title / meta pattern

- **Homepage title:** "Mobol — Aboriginal-owned digital agency in Perth | Web, Brand & AI"
- **Meta description pattern:** "Mobol is an Aboriginal-owned digital agency in Perth, WA. We design and build websites, brands, and AI integrations for businesses across Australia — honest scope, fair pricing, real partnership."
- **Service pages:** "[Service] in Perth | Mobol" — e.g. "Web Development in Perth | Mobol"

### Page-level rules

1. H1 includes a primary keyword **or** the brand promise, not both stuffed in.
2. First 100 words of any page mention Perth and one service term naturally.
3. Service pages: include "Perth," the service, and the audience in the first paragraph.
4. Use real WA-specific examples, suburbs, or industries on long-form pages (Fremantle, Joondalup, mining services, Noongar Country) — sparingly, where they fit.

## 12. Copy checklist (run on every page before ship)

- [ ] Does it sound calm, considered, modern, capable?
- [ ] Could a non-technical small business owner read this and understand it?
- [ ] Could a developer read this and not cringe?
- [ ] No banned words from section 9?
- [ ] Every adjective earned on the same page?
- [ ] No experience claims we can't back up (years, project counts, "world-class")?
- [ ] No apologetic framing either ("we're new," "just starting out")?
- [ ] If we mention Aboriginal identity, is it context — not pitch?
- [ ] Capitalisation correct (Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander, Country, Elder, Traditional Owner)?
- [ ] Acknowledgement of Country still present in footer with the Whadjuk Noongar wording?
- [ ] Perth is named where it should be (title, meta, footer, opening paragraph if relevant)?
- [ ] At least one specific (number, timeframe, outcome) per page?
- [ ] No promises the business can't keep ("24-hour response," "100% uptime")?
- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound like a person, not a brochure?
